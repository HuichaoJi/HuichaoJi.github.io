Rich Point-Light Percepts Are Not Specific to Biology:
Dynamic Point-Light Cloths



 Here are some demonstrations of the various conditions discussed in the following paper:
Erdogan, M., Bi, W., Yildirim, I., & Scholl, B. J. (2025). Dynamic point-light cloths generate rich percepts beyond biology. Current Biology, 35(12), 2967-2973.
These demonstrations are provided as MP4 files, which can be downloaded or viewed directly in most web-browsers. These movies are a bit large and choppy, but they should be sufficient to illustrate the basic conditions. As highly compressed versions of the original stimuli, these movies may not preserve the precise spatial and temporal characteristics of the originals.  
 
Visual processing seems specialized for perceiving other agents, as in biological motion perception: displays of surprisingly few moving dots ('point-light walkers'; PLWs) give rise to rich percepts of locomoting agents. Despite hundreds of experiments over decades of research, a foundational question remains unanswered: how specific are such phenomena to biological motion? Addressing this question has been historically challenging, largely due to the absence of non-biological comparison stimuli -- since the translation or rotation of rigid objects (as in 'structure-from-motion' displays) lacks the rich non-rigid relative motion of the points that is characteristic of PLWs. Here, to fill this gap, we introduce a novel visual phenomenon: the perception of rich behavior from dynamic point-light cloths (PLCs) -- as when a ribbon (or a sheet on a clothesline) is waving or floating in the wind. Across 13 preregistered experiments, while focusing on several of the most foundational properties of biological motion, we found broad similarities between PLWs and PLCs -- in terms of both experimental results and phenomenological demonstrations. These results collectively demonstrate that, even beyond agents and biology, the visual system extracts rich structure from surprisingly limited input.  
 
 
Spontaneous Perception (Experiments 1a-1f)
Animation S1.1A: A Point-light Cloth (Anchored) (1.4 MB)
 Observers readily perceive a cloth waving in the wind (anchored as if hanging on a clothesline) from only a small set of moving dots.  
 
Animation S1.1B: A Point-light Cloth (Unanchored) (304 KB)
 Observers similarly readily perceive a ribbon floating in the air (completely unanchored) from only a small set of moving dots.  
 
Animation S1.2A: A Scrambled Point-Light Cloth (Anchored) (1.5 MB)
 Animation S1.2B: A Scrambled Point-Light Cloth (Unanchored) (547 KB)
 These percepts critically depend on the holistic global arrangement of the moving dots, such that they are abolished when the very same motions are presented in spatially scrambled arrangements.

Holistic Perception vs. Local Motion (Experiment 3)
Animation S3.1: A 'Limited-Lifetime' Point-light Cloth (2.4 MB)
 Observers readily perceived a point-light cloth even from 'limited-lifetime' displays, in which a new random set of 16 dots are presented in every frame (thus preventing local tracking of individual dots). Compare what you see in the animation with what you see when stepping through it frame-by-frame!
